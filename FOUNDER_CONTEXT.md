# Project Rebuild

## Founder Context

> “We don’t build fitness apps. We build better decisions.”

## Vision

Project Rebuild is an AI-powered Decision Operating System.

Its purpose is not to count calories, track workouts, or build habits.

Its purpose is to help people make better decisions at the moments that matter most.

The product should reduce cognitive load, anticipate critical moments, and guide the user towards the next best decision.

## Why This Exists

Most people already know what they should do:

- Eat better.
- Train.
- Sleep more.
- Stop ordering takeout.
- Drink more water.

Knowledge is not the problem.

Execution is.

The gap between knowing and doing appears hundreds of times every week.

Project Rebuild exists to close that gap.

## The Core Idea

Every day is made of decisions.

Some have little impact. Others completely change the trajectory of the day.

Project Rebuild focuses only on the decisions that matter most, such as:

- Should I train today?
- Should I order Uber Eats?
- Should I go to bed now?
- Should I prepare tomorrow’s lunch?
- Should I reduce today’s workout because I slept badly?

The unit of the product is **one decision**.

Not a habit. Not a workout. Not a calorie.

A decision.

## The First User

The first user is the founder:

- 39 years old.
- A former Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu competitor.
- Previously trained consistently and identified as an athlete.
- Now has a demanding career, family responsibilities, limited time, inconsistent sleep, and reduced physical condition.
- Wants to reverse prediabetes and insulin resistance, reduce visceral fat, regain physical capacity, and eventually return to martial arts.
- Wants to remain emotionally present for his partner and be an active example for his son.

He does not lack information.

He needs a system that helps him make better decisions under real-world constraints.

The MVP will solve the founder’s actual problems first. If it works for him, it can later be generalized.

## Target Persona

The initial target persona is:

> Former athletes between 30 and 50 years old who became busy professionals and parents, lost consistency because of work and family demands, and want to recover their athletic identity.

They know what to do. They struggle to execute consistently.

Their real goal is not merely weight loss. It is to recognize themselves again as capable, energetic, and athletic people—adapted to the life they have now.

## Founder Mission

The founder’s mission is:

> Rebuild the athlete within, recover physical capacity, energy, and confidence, reverse metabolic decline, remain present for family, and become an active example for his son.

The product should support this mission without making health compete with family life.

## Product Philosophy

### Identity Before Outcomes

People do not only want to lose weight. They want to become—or become again—someone they recognize.

The product reinforces identity, never guilt.

### Decision Before Habit

Habits are the result. Decisions are the cause.

Improve enough decisions and useful habits emerge naturally.

### Context Beats Data

Weight alone means little.

Before recommending an action, the system should understand relevant context such as:

- Sleep.
- Stress.
- Schedule.
- Energy.
- Family demands.
- Workload.
- Available food.
- Previous behaviour.

### Reduce Cognitive Load

The product should simplify life, not add another system to manage.

The user should feel that the system thinks ahead and presents one clear next action.

### Progress Over Perfection

Missing one workout is acceptable. Abandoning the next aligned decision is not.

The system redirects without punishing, shaming, or demanding compensation.

### Family and Sustainability

The product must respect the user’s real priorities.

For the founder:

- Family has priority after work.
- Training should happen during the day whenever possible.
- The system must work after poor sleep, during demanding workdays, and while caring for a young child.
- A reduced action is better than a cancelled identity.

## Core Product Principles

1. **Decision First** — Every feature must improve a real decision.
2. **One Next Action** — Never overwhelm the user with a long list.
3. **Context Beats Data** — Recommendations must reflect the user’s current state.
4. **Identity Before Goals** — Reinforce who the user is rebuilding, not only numeric targets.
5. **Systems Over Motivation** — Reduce dependence on willpower.
6. **Automatic Before Manual** — Automate repeated context and timing wherever practical.
7. **Minimum Friction** — Actions should take seconds, not become administrative work.
8. **Adaptive Intelligence** — Learn which contexts, interventions, and language produce action.
9. **No Shame** — Diagnose system failures; never punish the user.
10. **Sustainable in Real Life** — The system must work on difficult days, not only ideal ones.

## The Product Hypothesis

If the system can identify critical decision moments and intervene with the right action at the right time, users will make better decisions with less mental effort.

Repeated better decisions will create consistency.

Consistency will rebuild identity and improve long-term outcomes.

## The AI’s Role

The AI is not a general-purpose chatbot.

It is a **Decision Coach**.

Its responsibilities are to:

- Understand the user’s current context.
- Anticipate moments of elevated risk or opportunity.
- Recommend one clear action.
- Explain briefly when explanation is useful.
- Learn from accepted, ignored, and rejected interventions.
- Adapt timing, tone, and recommendations over time.

The AI should communicate briefly, clearly, and respectfully.

It should not deliver motivational speeches when a concrete action is needed.

## Context and Operating States

The system should adapt to the user’s state rather than prescribing the same plan every day.

Initial operating states:

- **Performer** — Good sleep and energy; normal or challenging action.
- **Consistent** — Typical day; maintain the planned action.
- **Survival** — Poor sleep, high stress, or heavy family/work demand; reduce the action without abandoning it.
- **Recovery** — Illness, injury, or extreme fatigue; recovery becomes the correct decision.

Examples:

- If sleep was poor, replace an intense workout with P90X3, mobility, or a short walk.
- If a meeting is cancelled near lunchtime, present the workout as the immediate opportunity.
- If the user is likely to order takeout in the evening, intervene earlier by asking them to choose or prepare dinner.
- If the user misses a planned action, recommend the next aligned decision rather than compensation.

## Decision Score and Gamification

Gamification should reward the quality of decisions, not arbitrary engagement.

The primary unit is the **Decision Score**.

Examples of high-value decisions:

- Complete a planned workout.
- Choose food already available at home instead of ordering takeout.
- Prepare tomorrow’s lunch.
- Go to bed earlier when the opportunity exists.
- Replace an intense workout with an appropriate reduced session after poor sleep.

The system may use XP, streaks, levels, and progress summaries, but must avoid shame or manipulative loss mechanics.

Progress should emphasize identity:

- Restart.
- Momentum.
- Competitor.
- Athlete.
- Mentor.

Advancement should reflect consistent decisions over time, not weight alone.

## MVP Goal

The MVP validates one hypothesis:

> Can an AI-guided system improve real-world decisions at the moments that matter?

Nothing else matters until this is proven.

## MVP Experiences

### 1. Onboarding

Capture:

- Who were you at your best?
- Who are you today?
- Who do you want to become?
- What responsibilities and constraints shape your life?
- Which decisions most often derail you?
- What language and motivations resonate with you?

### 2. Today’s Decisions

Show only the three most important decisions for the current day.

Each decision should include:

- The action.
- The timing or trigger.
- Its Decision Score or XP value.
- A simple completion control.

### 3. Decision Score

Show decision quality and consistency without turning the product into a complex dashboard.

The score should make progress visible while keeping attention on the next useful action.

### 4. Decision Coach

Provide a context-aware conversation that knows:

- Today’s decisions.
- Recent behaviour.
- Current operating state.
- The user’s identity and mission.
- Relevant constraints.

The coach should recommend one next action rather than provide a broad lecture.

### 5. Timely Interventions

Send reminders before predictable decision moments.

Examples:

- Eat a pre-workout snack before a lunchtime session.
- Change into training clothes five minutes before the workout.
- Decide dinner before the end-of-day fatigue window.
- Begin the sleep routine when continued work is likely to harm recovery.

## Initial Founder Decision Moments

The first version should prioritize decisions already observed in the founder’s life:

1. Train during a protected lunchtime block on remote-work days.
2. Scale training intensity based on sleep and energy instead of cancelling.
3. Use prepared food or leftovers instead of ordering Uber Eats.
4. Decide dinner before 18:00.
5. Prevent late-night sweets through an earlier snack, a planned dinner, and a simple closing ritual.
6. Drink enough water throughout the day, particularly given a history of kidney stones.
7. Prepare food in advance to remove lunchtime and evening decision friction.
8. Stop work when continued work would reduce sleep without creating meaningful value.

## What Not to Build in the MVP

Do not build:

- Calorie counting.
- Detailed macro tracking.
- Complex health dashboards.
- Social feeds.
- Generic motivational quote libraries.
- Achievements for every action.
- Broad vendor-by-vendor wearable integrations without a clear decision use
  case. After the decision loop was validated, the founder authorized a narrow
  Health Data Bridge through Apple HealthKit / Android Health Connect, with
  provenance, granular consent and no complex health dashboard.
- A large settings surface.
- Features copied from other fitness apps without a clear decision use case.

Complexity is the enemy.

## Success Metrics

The MVP succeeds if users:

- Make more high-quality decisions.
- Complete more planned actions.
- Recover faster after missed actions.
- Reduce high-risk behaviours such as unplanned takeout.
- Report lower decision fatigue.
- Find interventions timely and useful rather than annoying.

Weight loss, fitness, and health improvements are important outcomes, but they are lagging consequences—not the primary MVP KPI.

## Engineering Principles

The implementation should remain:

- Modular.
- AI-provider agnostic where practical.
- Easy to evolve.
- Easy to test.
- Clearly documented.
- Privacy-conscious.
- Simple enough for rapid iteration.

Avoid premature optimization.

Prefer clarity over cleverness.

The initial implementation is expected to use a web-first stack suitable for deployment on Vercel, with the option to become a Progressive Web App.

## Canonical Product Rule

Before implementing any feature, ask:

> What real decision becomes easier or better because of this?

If the answer is unclear, do not build it.

## Long-Term Vision

Project Rebuild should become an AI-powered Decision Operating System that helps people become who they want to be by improving the decisions that shape their days.

Health is the first domain.

The same core Decision Engine may later support:

- Productivity.
- Finances.
- Parenting.
- Learning.
- Relationships.

The company is not building another health tracker.

It is building a system that reduces cognitive load and helps people make better decisions when those decisions matter most.
