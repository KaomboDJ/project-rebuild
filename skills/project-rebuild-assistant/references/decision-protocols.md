# Decision protocols

## Determine the daily state

- **Performer:** good sleep and energy; complete the planned session.
- **Consistent:** ordinary day; complete the standard 30-minute session.
- **Survival:** poor sleep or heavy stress; use a shorter P90X3 session, mobility, or a 15–20 minute walk.
- **Recovery:** illness, injury, or extreme exhaustion; prioritize hydration, food quality, rest, and medical care when needed.

Never use intense exercise to compensate for food.

## Lunchtime training

Default remote-work sequence:

1. Eat a normal breakfast around 09:00.
2. At 11:15, use a light pre-workout only if hungry or breakfast was insufficient: banana with whey, or yogurt with fruit.
3. At 11:55, change into training clothes.
4. Train around 12:00 for 30 minutes.
5. Shower and eat a prepared lunch.

If meetings block the session, move it once to the next realistic daytime slot. If no slot remains, use the Survival fallback.

## Takeout prevention

Intervene before fatigue peaks:

1. Decide dinner before 18:00.
2. Check prepared food, leftovers, eggs, tuna, soup, or frozen meals.
3. If food exists, recommend the fastest adequate meal.
4. If nothing exists, use a pre-approved simple order rather than an impulsive high-calorie order.

Do not wait until the user has already opened the delivery app.

## Night cravings

1. Confirm whether it is physical hunger, craving, habit, or stress.
2. If physical hunger: choose a planned protein-forward option, such as yogurt or whey, within medical/dietary constraints.
3. If craving or habit: water or unsweetened tea, brush teeth, leave the kitchen, wait 10 minutes.
4. If the user still chooses a sweet, recommend one served portion without punishment or compensation.

## Poor sleep

- Do not prescribe Insanity after very poor sleep.
- Prefer strength at reduced volume, mobility, or walking.
- Keep meals regular; do not chase energy with sugar.
- Encourage the earliest realistic bedtime opportunity.

## Hydration

Because of the reported kidney-stone history, prioritize regular hydration across the day rather than large boluses.

Do not prescribe a fixed high volume without considering medical advice, climate, exercise, kidney/heart conditions, and urine output. Encourage clinician guidance on stone type and prevention.

## Prediabetes and insulin resistance

Prioritize:

- Clinician follow-up and repeat laboratory monitoring.
- Weight reduction through a sustainable calorie deficit.
- Regular resistance training and walking.
- Whole fruit rather than juice.
- Fiber-rich foods and protein distributed across meals.
- Reduced sugary drinks, sweets, and unplanned late-night eating.

Do not diagnose, promise reversal, or recommend medication/supplement changes without medical oversight.

## Missed action

Ask what blocked execution:

- Timing.
- Sleep.
- Meetings.
- Food preparation.
- Family need.
- Avoidance/distraction.

Change one part of the system. Do not redesign the whole plan after one miss.
